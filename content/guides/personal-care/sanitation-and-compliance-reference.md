## What This Guide Covers
- How to turn your sanitation and safety protocols into a searchable digital reference, not a binder nobody opens
- How to use Claude to write clear, specific protocol entries in plain language
- How to keep it current as regulations or your own procedures change

---

## Why This Matters
Sanitation protocols matter for client safety and for meeting licensing requirements, but a printed binder tends to get consulted once and then never again. A searchable digital version solves the actual problem, finding the answer in seconds when it's needed.

---

## Case Study: Petal and Stone Spa
Petal and Stone Spa had a sanitation binder that stayed in a back office drawer, rarely opened. The owner used Claude to turn the same protocols into a searchable digital reference tool, organized by treatment type.

**Result:** New staff started actually using the reference during their first few shifts instead of asking a coworker or guessing.

---

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Gather your existing protocols
Pull together whatever documentation you currently have.

### Step 2: Write clear protocol entries with Claude

```
Here are my rough notes on a sanitation protocol: [describe the 
procedure in your own words]

Write a clear, step by step version of this protocol in plain 
language, the way you'd explain it to a new employee on their 
first day.
```

### Step 3: Prompt Claude to build a searchable reference tool

```
Build a searchable reference tool as an interactive webpage for a 
[type of business]'s sanitation and safety protocols.

Categories: [list your categories, e.g. tools and equipment, 
treatment rooms, linens, hand hygiene]
Protocols: [paste your written protocols, organized by category]

The tool should:
- Let me search or filter by category or keyword
- Display each protocol clearly with numbered steps
- Use a simple, minimal visual style with [your brand color, e.g. 
navy blue] as the primary accent color
```

### Step 4: Have a staff member test it cold
Ask someone unfamiliar with the tool to find a specific protocol without help.

### Step 5: Keep it accessible during shifts
Bookmark the tool on any shared device.

### Step 6: Revisit it when anything changes
Update the specific protocol whenever a regulation or your own procedure changes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does this replace formal licensing or compliance training?**
No. This organizes what you already know and are required to follow.

**Q: How do I know if my protocols are actually compliant with regulations?**
Check with your licensing board or a compliance resource specific to your state and profession.

**Q: Should this be visible to clients?**
Many businesses keep this as an internal staff tool.

**Q: What if I need to update a protocol quickly during a shift?**
Update the tool by returning to the same conversation, though for anything urgent, a verbal update to staff matters more.
