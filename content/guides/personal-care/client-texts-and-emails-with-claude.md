## What This Guide Covers
- How to handle routine client messages, questions, rescheduling requests, and general inquiries, faster and more clearly
- How to use Claude to match your tone across every message so clients get a consistent experience
- How to build a short reference set of go to responses for the messages you get most often

---

## Why This Matters
Most of the messages a personal care business sends aren't marketing, they're the day to day back and forth: answering a pricing question, confirming a reschedule, clarifying what to expect at a first appointment. These messages happen constantly, often written in a rush between clients.

---

## Case Study: Sable Row Barbershop
Sable Row Barbershop found that new client questions about walk in availability and pricing were eating up time throughout the day, often answered slightly differently depending on who replied. The owner used Claude to build a short set of standard responses for the five most common questions.

**Result:** Response times dropped, and the shop's answers stayed consistent no matter who picked up the message.

---

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: List the messages you get most often
Spend a few days noticing the actual questions and requests that come in repeatedly.

### Step 2: Write your core response set with Claude

```
Here are the questions I get most often from clients, with rough 
notes on how I usually answer:

1. [question]: [your usual answer, in your own words]
2. [question]: [your usual answer]
[continue as needed]

Write a clean, clear version of each response, under 300 
characters where possible, in a tone that is [describe your tone, 
e.g. friendly but efficient].
```

### Step 3: Handle a tricky message in the moment

```
A client just messaged me: [paste or describe the message].
Here's the context I need to respond with: [context].

Draft a reply in my usual tone: [describe your tone].
```

### Step 4: Adapt the same response set for email

```
Take these text responses and write slightly longer email versions 
where it makes sense, adding a touch more detail without becoming 
formal or stiff.

[paste your response set]
```

### Step 5: Share the set with your team, if you have one
A shared reference set keeps the business's voice consistent no matter who's responding.

### Step 6: Update it as new questions come up
Add to the set whenever a new question starts repeating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Doesn't this make my responses feel less personal?**
Not if you use it as a starting point rather than a script.

**Q: What if a client's question needs a judgment call, not a standard answer?**
Those are exactly the messages worth writing carefully rather than pulling from a template.

**Q: Should I use the exact same responses across text and email?**
The core information should stay the same, but email can carry slightly more detail.

**Q: How often should I update my response set?**
Whenever a new type of question starts coming up repeatedly.
