## Why This Matters
Personal care businesses tend to have real seasonal patterns: wedding season, holiday party season, summer skin concerns, back to school haircuts. Clients are already thinking about these moments, which makes seasonal promotions land more naturally than a generic discount pulled out of nowhere.

The challenge is usually turning one idea into everything needed to run it: a caption, an email, maybe a sign for the front desk, all saying roughly the same thing in a way that fits each format.

---

## Case Study: The Quiet Chair
The Quiet Chair, a hair salon, wanted to run a promotion ahead of wedding season. Using Claude, the owner turned a single idea, a bridal party package, into an Instagram caption, a Google Business post, a short email, and a description for her booking site, all in one afternoon.

**Result:** The salon booked three full bridal parties that season, more than in any previous year.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Map out your natural seasonal moments

```
I run a [type of personal care business]. Based on typical client 
needs and behavior for this type of business, suggest 6 seasonal 
or calendar moments throughout the year that would make sense to 
build a promotion around, with a one line reason for each.
```

### Step 2: Pick one moment and describe the offer
Choose the promotion closest on the calendar and get specific about the price, timeframe, and who it's for.

### Step 3: Generate the full set of materials from one prompt

```
I'm running a promotion: [describe the offer, timeframe, and who 
it's for].

Generate a coordinated set of materials:
1. An Instagram caption, under 100 words
2. A Google Business post, under 1500 characters
3. A short promotional email with subject line
4. A one paragraph description for my booking page

Keep the tone consistent across all four, and make sure they all 
mention the actual details of the offer accurately.
```

### Step 4: Review for consistency and accuracy
Check that dates, prices, and terms match exactly across every version.

### Step 5: Build a simple seasonal calendar for the year ahead

```
Based on the 6 seasonal moments you suggested earlier, build a 
simple calendar showing roughly when I should start planning and 
promoting each one, working backward from the seasonal date.
```

### Step 6: Reuse and adjust each year
Save your best performing campaigns and revisit them next year with small updates.

*Tip:* If you saved last year's materials, upload them into your Claude conversation and ask for an updated version rather than starting over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How far in advance should I start promoting a seasonal campaign?**
For something tied to a planned event like a wedding, several weeks to a couple of months ahead is reasonable. For more spontaneous seasonal moments, one to two weeks is often enough.

**Q: What if my business doesn't have obvious seasonal patterns?**
Almost every personal care business has some rhythm, even if it's just slower summer months or a holiday rush.

**Q: Should every campaign include a discount?**
No. Some of the strongest seasonal campaigns are about awareness or a limited time offer, like extended hours or a special add on, rather than always cutting price.

**Q: How many seasonal campaigns should I run per year?**
Four to six well planned campaigns tend to work better than trying to build something around every possible holiday.
