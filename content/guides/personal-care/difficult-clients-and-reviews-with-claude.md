## Why This Matters
Difficult client situations are one of the few places where the wrong wording genuinely costs a business real relationships, and often other people see it too, in the case of a public review.

A calm, specific, non defensive response usually resolves more than people expect.

---

## Case Study: Harlow Skin Studio
Harlow Skin Studio received a negative online review from a client unhappy with a facial that didn't meet their expectations. The owner drafted a response with Claude that acknowledged the specific concern without getting defensive.

**Result:** The client updated their review after a follow up conversation.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Read the message or review fully before reacting

> Never respond to a negative review or upset client message in the heat of the moment. A response written while frustrated is very hard to take back once it's public or sent.

### Step 2: Draft a response with Claude

```
A client left this review or message: [paste the review or 
describe the situation accurately]

Here's context that's relevant: [add any context]

Draft a calm, non defensive response that acknowledges their 
concern specifically, doesn't over apologize or admit fault I 
don't believe I have, and offers a clear next step. Keep it under 
150 words.
```

### Step 3: Push back on anything that sounds defensive
If any line sounds like it's arguing with the client, ask Claude to soften it.

### Step 4: Handle an in person or live message situation

```
A client is upset about [describe the situation]. I want to stay 
calm and fair without just giving in to avoid conflict. Give me 
two or three things I could say that acknowledge their frustration 
and move toward a resolution.
```

### Step 5: Decide when to escalate beyond a drafted response
Some situations need your own judgment or outside advice rather than a quick reply.

### Step 6: Follow up after the situation is resolved
A short, genuine follow up can turn a rocky interaction into a client who stays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Should I ever delete or hide a negative review?**
Most platforms don't allow you to delete reviews you disagree with, only report ones that violate their policies.

**Q: What if the review is unfair or based on a misunderstanding?**
You can still respond factually and calmly without being defensive.

**Q: How fast should I respond to a negative review?**
Within a day or two is reasonable.

**Q: What if a client's behavior crosses into being abusive, not just upset?**
That's a different situation, and it's reasonable to set a boundary directly.
