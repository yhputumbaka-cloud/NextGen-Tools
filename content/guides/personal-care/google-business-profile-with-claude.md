## Why This Matters
When someone searches "hair salon near me" or "barber open now," Google Business Profile decides whether you show up at all, and whether the listing looks credible enough to tap.

Most independent personal care businesses claim their profile once and stop there:
- No description, or one that reads like it was written for a different business
- A handful of outdated photos, sometimes years old
- Hours that haven't been accurate since last season

Google reads an incomplete profile as a sign the business might not be very active, and shows it less often. A complete, current profile does the opposite. It signals that you're open, real, and worth showing to someone nearby who needs what you do.

The full setup takes about an hour, and you only do it once. After that, a few small updates a month keep it performing.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Claim or find your existing profile
Search your business name on Google.
- Already shows up? Click "Own this business" to claim it.
- Nothing shows up? Go to google.com/business and create a new listing.

Verification usually happens by phone, text, or a postcard mailed to your business address. Finish verification before moving on to anything else in this guide.

### Step 2: Get the basics exactly right
Before writing anything, go through every field in your dashboard:
- Your most specific category, such as "Hair Salon" or "Massage Therapist," not just "Beauty Salon"
- Your service area, if you travel to clients rather than working from a fixed location
- Your actual hours, including any lunch breaks or days that are appointment only

These fields affect when and how often you show up in search, so accuracy matters more than polish here.

### Step 3: Write your business description with Claude
Your description has a limited character count and needs to say something real about why someone should choose you.

Use this prompt, filling in your own details:

```
Write a Google Business Profile description for my business.
Here are the details:

Business name: [business name]
Type of business: [e.g. independent hair salon, day spa, massage therapist]
Location: [neighborhood or city]
Specialties: [e.g. curly hair cuts, balayage, deep tissue massage]
What makes it different: [1 to 2 things]

Keep it under 750 characters, write in third person, and avoid 
generic phrases like "passion for beauty" or "we treat you like family."
```

Read what Claude gives you and push back on specific lines rather than asking for a full rewrite. "Make the second sentence more specific" gets you a better result than starting over each time.

### Step 4: Build out your services list
Google lets you list individual services with short descriptions and prices, and it can surface you when someone searches a specific service by name.

```
Here is my full list of services and prices:
[paste your service menu]

Write a one sentence description for each service, as it would 
appear on my Google Business Profile. Keep the tone straightforward 
and specific, and don't repeat the same three or four adjectives 
across every description.
```

### Step 5: Add photos that actually convert
Profiles with more photos get more views, but the photos need to earn their place. Upload:
- Your storefront, entrance, or workspace
- A few before and after or finished result shots, if your work is visual
- A photo of you or your team

Set your strongest photo as the cover image, since that's what shows up first in search results.

*Tip:* If a photo feels flat or poorly lit, upload it directly into your Claude conversation and ask what's off and how to improve the lighting next time. Claude can look at the actual image and give specific feedback, not generic photography advice.

### Step 6: Turn on messaging and keep the profile active
Turn on the messaging feature so clients can reach you directly from the listing, and check the Q&A section regularly. Anyone can post a question there, and if you don't answer it, a stranger might answer it wrong.

Once a month, add a new photo and post a short update.

```
Write a short Google Business Profile post announcing [what's new, 
e.g. a new service, extended hours, a seasonal offer]. Keep it 
under 1500 characters, direct and specific.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a physical storefront to have a Google Business Profile?**
No. If you work from a private studio, a rented chair, or travel to clients, you can still create a profile and set a service area instead of a public address.

**Q: How long does verification usually take?**
Phone or text verification can be instant. A mailed postcard typically takes one to two weeks, so start early if you're launching a new profile ahead of an opening date.

**Q: Should I respond to reviews myself, or use Claude for that too?**
Claude can help draft responses, especially for reviews that need a careful, non defensive tone. That's covered in a separate guide in the Client Relationships and Booking category.

**Q: What if my business has multiple practitioners under one roof?**
You can run one shared profile and use the description and Posts features to highlight individual team members and their specialties.
