## Why This Matters
Before and after photos are the single most persuasive marketing tool most personal care businesses have. A strong photo with a weak caption undersells the work, and "loved doing this transformation" tells a potential client nothing about what actually happened or why it took skill.

The businesses that get the most out of their visual work write captions that briefly explain the process or the challenge, which helps a potential client understand what they're actually paying for.

---

## Case Study: Golden Hour Skincare
Golden Hour Skincare, a solo esthetician practice, had strong before and after photos with captions that were one line long and repetitive. The owner started describing the actual protocol used for each result to Claude and turning that into captions for Instagram and Facebook.

**Result:** Engagement on posts increased, and several new clients mentioned specific treatments they'd seen described in a caption when they booked.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Describe what actually happened in the photo
Before writing a caption, note the real details: what the client wanted, what you did, and roughly how long it took.

**Tip:** You can also upload the actual before and after photo directly into your Claude conversation along with those notes. Claude can see the photo and often pulls out specific visual details worth mentioning that you wouldn't have thought to describe yourself.

### Step 2: Write the first draft with Claude

```
Here is what happened in this before and after: the client wanted 
[goal]. I used [technique/product/process]. It took about [time]. 
The result was [describe outcome].

Write an Instagram caption, under 100 words, that explains this in 
a way a non expert would find genuinely interesting. Avoid phrases 
like "swipe to see the transformation" or "loved this look."
```

### Step 3: Adapt the caption for other platforms

```
Take this Instagram caption and write two more versions: one 
slightly longer for Facebook that adds more detail about the 
process, and one under 150 characters for a Google Business post.

[paste your Instagram caption]
```

### Step 4: Add relevant hashtags, if your platform uses them

```
Suggest 8 to 10 hashtags for this post: a mix of broad terms 
related to [your service, e.g. balayage, deep tissue massage] and 
specific local terms for [your city or neighborhood].

[paste your caption]
```

### Step 5: Build a reusable caption structure

```
Based on the captions I've written so far, create three reusable 
caption templates I can use for future before and after posts, 
with bracketed sections for the details that change each time.
```

### Step 6: Batch write captions for a backlog of photos
If you have a folder of finished work without captions, tackle it in one sitting.

```
I'm going to describe five before and after results in a row. 
Write a short caption for each, using a similar structure but 
varied enough that they don't feel repetitive.

1. [describe result 1]
2. [describe result 2]
[continue as needed]
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if the before photo isn't flattering to show?**
Crop tightly to focus on the specific area of the result, or lean on client wording about how they felt rather than showing an unflattering full before photo.

**Q: Do I need client permission to post before and after photos?**
Yes. Get clear consent before posting any client photo, and be specific about where it will be used.

**Q: How technical should the caption get?**
Specific enough to sound credible, simple enough that someone with no background in your field can follow it.

**Q: Should every post be a before and after?**
No. Mixing in process shots and non visual posts like tips or FAQs keeps your page from feeling like a constant sales pitch.
