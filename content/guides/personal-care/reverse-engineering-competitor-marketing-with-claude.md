## Why This Matters
It's easy to assume that because a competitor is busy, everything they're doing must be working. In reality, most personal care businesses are marketing on instinct, not strategy, which means there's usually real opportunity in noticing what they're missing rather than only what they're doing well.

Looking closely at a handful of similar businesses, their Google profiles, their social presence, their reviews, can surface ideas you wouldn't have thought of on your own, and can reveal gaps you could fill.

---

## Case Study: Willow and Co
Willow and Co, a small day spa, reviewed the Google profiles and Instagram pages of four nearby competitors and fed the notes into Claude. The analysis flagged that none of the four were using the Google Posts feature, and three had outdated service lists.

**Result:** The owner used that gap to keep her own profile more current, and began noticing more calls referencing specific services listed on her page.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Choose 3 to 5 real competitors to review
Pick businesses genuinely similar to yours in size and service type, ideally in your general area, rather than only the biggest, most established names.

### Step 2: Gather notes on each one
For each competitor, note how often they post, what their Google profile looks like, common themes in their reviews, and anything that stands out.

*Tip:* Instead of typing everything out by hand, take screenshots of each competitor's profile and reviews and upload them directly to Claude.

### Step 3: Ask Claude to find patterns across what you observed

```
Here are my notes on four competitors in my area:

Competitor 1: [notes]
Competitor 2: [notes]
Competitor 3: [notes]
Competitor 4: [notes]

Based on these notes, what patterns do you see across all of them? 
What are they all doing well? What is everyone missing?
```

### Step 4: Ask for gap based ideas specific to your business

```
Based on the gaps you identified, suggest 5 specific marketing 
ideas for my business, [business name and type], that take 
advantage of what competitors are missing.
```

### Step 5: Read reviews for language patterns, not just star ratings

```
Here are excerpts from customer reviews across a few competitors: 
[paste a handful of review snippets or your own summary]

What are clients consistently praising or complaining about? What 
does this suggest people in my area value most?
```

### Step 6: Turn the strongest ideas into a short action list
Narrow the ideas down to two or three you can realistically act on this month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it ethical to look closely at what competitors are doing?**
Yes. Looking at publicly available profiles and reviews is standard business research. Directly copying specific content, photos, or language is the line to avoid.

**Q: How often should I do this kind of review?**
Once or twice a year is usually enough to stay aware of shifts in your local market.

**Q: What if I don't have many local competitors to look at?**
Widen your search to a nearby city or a similar business type elsewhere, since the goal is pattern recognition, not a strict local comparison.

**Q: Should I ask Claude to browse competitor websites directly?**
This guide is built around you gathering the raw observations yourself, or capturing them as screenshots, and using Claude to analyze them.
