## What This Guide Covers
- How to set up an **automated email or text sequence** that sends itself, instead of manually sending every promo
- How to use Claude to **write the message content** that goes inside that automation, a welcome series, a win back message, a birthday note
- How to choose the **right trigger** so messages go out based on client behavior, not a date you have to remember

---

## Why This Matters
Manually sending promos requires you to remember on the right day, write something, and actually hit send. That's a task that gets dropped during a busy week, and the client list you worked to build sits unused.

An automated sequence solves this at the root. You set it up once inside your email or texting platform, and it keeps running whether or not you're thinking about it that week. A welcome message that fires the moment someone joins your list. A win back message that goes out automatically to anyone who hasn't booked in 45 days. Neither needs you to remember anything.

**Claude's role in this guide is the content, not the infrastructure.** The actual sending and triggering happens inside a platform like Mailchimp or MailerLite. Claude writes what the message says. The platform decides when it goes out.

The Fade Room set up an automated win back sequence in MailerLite that fires for any client who hasn't booked in 45 days, using message content written and tested with Claude before it went live.

**Result:** The sequence kept running on its own for months afterward, generating rebookings the owner hadn't manually followed up on.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Pick one sequence to start with
Don't try to build every automation at once. A welcome series for new subscribers or a win back sequence for lapsed clients are the two with the clearest return for most personal care businesses.

### Step 2: Set up the trigger inside your platform
In Mailchimp or MailerLite, this lives under an area usually called Automations or Journeys. Set the trigger, such as "tag added" for a welcome series, or "no booking in 45 days" for a win back sequence, before writing any content.

### Step 3: Write the message content with Claude

```
Write a [welcome/win back] message for my [type of business].

Context: [e.g. this fires automatically 45 days after a client's 
last booking]
Tone: [describe your brand voice]

Keep it under 150 words for email, or under 320 characters if 
it's a text step. Make it sound like a real message from a real 
person, not an automated notice.
```

### Step 4: Set the timing between steps
If your sequence has more than one message, decide the delay between each. A welcome series might send immediately, then again three days later. Space steps out enough that they don't feel like a burst of spam.

### Step 5: Test the automation before it goes live
Send yourself a test version and check the trigger fires correctly, not just that the content reads well.

> Never turn on a new automation without testing it on yourself first. A trigger set up wrong can send the wrong message to every client on your list at once.

### Step 6: Check performance monthly instead of managing it daily
Once it's running, a quick monthly glance at open rates or booking follow through is enough. The whole point is that you're no longer sending each one by hand.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a paid platform to set up automation like this?**
Basic automation is available on the free tier of some platforms for small lists, though features and limits change, so check your specific platform's current plan details before assuming.

**Q: What if my booking platform handles texting instead of a separate email tool?**
Some booking platforms have their own automation for client messaging. Check there first before adding a separate tool.

**Q: How many messages should be in a sequence?**
Two or three is usually enough. More than that starts to feel like a lot from a small business a client just started hearing from.

**Q: Will this feel impersonal since it's automated?**
Not if the writing sounds specific and human, which is exactly what Claude is helping with in Step 3. The automation handles timing, not tone.
