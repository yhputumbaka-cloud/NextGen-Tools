## What This Guide Covers
- How to handle routine member messages, questions about classes, membership changes, and general inquiries, faster and more clearly
- How to use Claude to match your tone across every message so members get a consistent experience
- How to build a short reference set of go to responses for the messages you get most often

---

## Why This Matters
Most of the messages a gym or studio sends aren't marketing, they're the day to day back and forth. Claude is useful here less as a writer of anything elaborate, and more as a fast, reliable second pass.

---

## Case Study: Ironhide Barbell Club
Ironhide Barbell Club found that new member questions about what to bring to a first class and how membership freezes worked were eating up time throughout the day, often answered slightly differently depending on which coach replied.

**Result:** Response times dropped, and the gym's answers stayed consistent no matter which coach picked up the message.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: List the messages you get most often

### Step 2: Write your core response set with Claude

```
Here are the questions I get most often from members, with rough 
notes on how I usually answer:

1. [question]: [your usual answer, in your own words]
2. [question]: [your usual answer]
[continue as needed]

Write a clean, clear version of each response, under 300 
characters where possible, in a tone that is [describe your tone, 
e.g. direct and encouraging].
```

### Step 3: Handle a tricky message in the moment

```
A member just messaged me: [paste or describe the message].
Here's the context I need to respond with: [context].

Draft a reply in my usual tone: [describe your tone].
```

### Step 4: Adapt the same response set for email

```
Take these text responses and write slightly longer email versions 
where it makes sense, adding a touch more detail without becoming 
formal or stiff.

[paste your response set]
```

### Step 5: Share the set with your coaching team

*Tip:* Keep this set somewhere quick to access mid shift, like a pinned note on your phone.

### Step 6: Update it as new questions come up

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Doesn't this make my responses feel less personal?**
Not if you use it as a starting point rather than a script.

**Q: What if a member's question involves a billing dispute or something sensitive?**
Those are exactly the messages worth writing carefully rather than pulling from a template.

**Q: Should I use the exact same responses across text and email?**
The core information should stay the same, but email can carry slightly more detail.

**Q: How often should I update my response set?**
Whenever a new type of question starts coming up repeatedly.
