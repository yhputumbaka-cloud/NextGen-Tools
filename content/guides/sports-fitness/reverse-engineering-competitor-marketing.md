## What This Guide Covers

- How to use Claude to spot patterns and gaps across a handful of competitor profiles
- How to turn what you learn into ideas that are genuinely yours, not imitations

---

## Why This Matters
It's easy to assume that because a competitor's classes are full, everything they're doing must be working. Looking closely at a handful of similar businesses can surface ideas you wouldn't have thought of on your own.

---

## Case Study: Tidal Pilates Studio
Tidal Pilates Studio reviewed the Google profiles and Instagram pages of four nearby studios and fed the notes into Claude. The analysis flagged that none of the four clearly listed their free trial offer anywhere visible.

**Result:** The owner made sure her own trial offer was prominent everywhere, and began noticing more first time bookings specifically mentioning it.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Choose 3 to 5 real competitors to review

### Step 2: Gather notes on each one

*Tip:* Instead of typing everything out by hand, take screenshots of each competitor's profile and reviews and upload them directly to Claude.

### Step 3: Ask Claude to find patterns across what you observed

```
Here are my notes on four competitors in my area:

Competitor 1: [notes]
Competitor 2: [notes]
Competitor 3: [notes]
Competitor 4: [notes]

Based on these notes, what patterns do you see across all of them? 
What are they all doing well? What is everyone missing?
```

### Step 4: Ask for gap based ideas specific to your business

```
Based on the gaps you identified, suggest 5 specific marketing 
ideas for my business, [business name and type], that take 
advantage of what competitors are missing.
```

### Step 5: Read reviews for language patterns, not just star ratings

```
Here are excerpts from customer reviews across a few competitors: 
[paste a handful of review snippets or your own summary]

What are members consistently praising or complaining about?
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it ethical to look closely at what competitors are doing?**
Yes, looking at publicly available profiles and reviews is standard business research.

**Q: How often should I do this kind of review?**
Once or twice a year is usually enough.

**Q: What if I don't have many local competitors to look at?**
Widen your search to a nearby city or a similar business type elsewhere.

**Q: Should I ask Claude to browse competitor websites directly?**
This guide is built around you gathering the raw observations yourself.
