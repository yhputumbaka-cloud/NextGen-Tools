## What This Guide Covers
- How to find **current, credible industry benchmarks** for food cost, labor cost, and prime cost
- How to compare your own numbers against those benchmarks honestly
- How to use the comparison to identify whether a number that feels normal to you is **actually high, low, or right in line**

---

## Why This Matters
Without a point of comparison, it's hard to know whether your own numbers are actually good. Claude can search for current industry figures directly, rather than relying on outdated numbers from memory, and help you compare your actual situation against them honestly.

## Case Study: Brookline Bakehouse
The owner of Brookline Bakehouse had never compared the bakery's food cost percentage against any outside benchmark. After asking Claude to find current industry benchmarks for a bakery-cafe operation, it became clear food cost was running meaningfully higher than typical.

**Result:** The comparison prompted a deeper look at ingredient sourcing, leading to a switched supplier that brought food cost percentage down closer to the benchmark range within two months.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Gather Your Own Real Numbers
Know your own current food cost percentage, labor cost percentage, and prime cost.

### Step 2: Ask Claude to Find Current Benchmarks

```
Search for current industry benchmark figures for food cost
percentage, labor cost percentage, and prime cost for a [your
restaurant type, e.g. casual dining, quick service, fine dining]
restaurant. I want recent, credible sources, not outdated general
rules of thumb.
```

### Step 3: Compare Your Numbers Honestly

```
Here are my actual numbers: food cost is [your percentage], labor
cost is [your percentage], prime cost is [your percentage]. How do
these compare to the benchmarks you found? Be direct about where
I'm running higher, lower, or in line.
```

### Step 4: Understand Why a Gap Might Exist

```
If my food cost is running higher than the benchmark, what are the
most common reasons for that in a restaurant like mine, and which
of those seem most likely to apply to my specific situation?
```

### Step 5: Decide What's Actually Worth Investigating

```
Based on this comparison, which of my numbers is the most worth
digging into further, and why? I want to prioritize where to focus,
not try to fix everything at once.
```

### Step 6: Re-Benchmark Periodically
Revisit this comparison every six months to a year, using Claude's live search each time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How current are the benchmarks Claude finds?**
Since Claude searches the web directly, the figures reflect genuinely current sources, always check the source and date cited.

**Q: What if my numbers are below benchmark? Is that automatically good?**
Not necessarily, unusually low food cost could reflect smart sourcing or portion sizes smaller than customers expect.

**Q: Do benchmarks vary a lot by restaurant type?**
Significantly, always specify your actual restaurant type when asking for benchmarks.

**Q: Is prime cost more useful than looking at food cost and labor cost separately?**
Many operators consider prime cost the more meaningful combined number.

**Q: What if I don't have accurate numbers to compare in the first place?**
Work through the earlier guides in this category first to build confidence in your own real numbers.
