## What This Guide Covers
- How to **upload your review exports directly to Claude** instead of manually copying and pasting each one
- How to spot **real patterns** across dozens of reviews instead of reacting to whichever one you read most recently
- How to turn what you find into a **short, specific list of changes** you can actually brief your team on

---

## Why This Matters
Most restaurant owners read reviews reactively, a great one makes their day, a bad one ruins it, and neither moment usually leads to a real change. The actual value in reviews isn't any single one of them, it's the pattern across many of them over time.

---

## Case Study: Driftwood Café
The owner of Driftwood Café had a strong four-and-a-half star average but had never systematically looked across all the reviews together. After exporting six months of Google and Yelp reviews and uploading them directly into a conversation with Claude, a clear pattern emerged, multiple reviews mentioned a long wait for coffee refills during weekend brunch.

**Result:** A small staffing adjustment led to several reviews in the following months explicitly noting the improvement.

---

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Export Your Reviews
Gather as many recent reviews as you reasonably can, ideally the last three to six months.

### Step 2: Upload Directly Into Claude

```
I've uploaded our recent customer reviews from [platform/timeframe].
Read through all of them and identify the most common themes, both
positive and negative. I'm looking for genuine patterns, not just a
summary of individual reviews.
```

### Step 3: Ask for Specificity, Not Generalities

```
For the negative patterns you found, pull a few specific examples
or phrases from the actual reviews that illustrate each one.
```

### Step 4: Separate What's Fixable From What Isn't

```
Of the patterns you found, which ones are things we could realistically
change, versus things that are more about personal taste or one-off
circumstances we can't really control?
```

### Step 5: Build the Action Plan

```
Based on the fixable patterns we identified, help me build a short
action plan, no more than three to five specific changes, ranked by
how much impact they're likely to have versus how hard they'd be to
implement.
```

### Step 6: Turn It Into a Staff Briefing

```
Now turn this action plan into a short briefing I can share with my
team, explaining what we're changing and why, based on what customers
have actually been telling us.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How many reviews do I need before this kind of analysis is actually useful?**
Patterns become meaningfully reliable somewhere around twenty to thirty reviews.

**Q: Should I do this every month, or less often?**
Monthly is a reasonable rhythm for most independent restaurants.

**Q: What if the reviews reveal something genuinely upsetting, like a complaint about a specific staff member?**
Treat that as a private operational conversation, not something to address in a general team briefing.

**Q: Can this same process work for written customer surveys instead of public reviews?**
Yes, the same upload-and-analyze approach works for any batch of written customer feedback.

**Q: What if the patterns Claude finds don't match what I personally believed customers thought?**
That gap is often the most valuable finding in the whole exercise.
