## What This Guide Covers
- How to upload a vendor contract or invoice and have Claude **flag unusual terms or unexpected changes**
- How to calculate the **real impact of a price change** rather than just noticing a number went up
- How to walk into a vendor conversation with **specific, informed questions** instead of a vague sense something feels off

---

## Why This Matters
Vendor contracts and invoices are dense, repetitive, and easy to skim past. That's exactly how a quietly unfavorable term or a gradual price creep goes unnoticed for months. Claude can read through a contract or invoice directly, flag what's unusual, and help you understand the actual financial impact in plain language.

---

## Case Study: Brookline Bakehouse
The owner of Brookline Bakehouse had been with the same flour and dairy supplier for years and assumed the relationship was still a fair deal. Uploading a recent invoice and the original signed contract revealed that delivery fees, originally waived, had quietly become a standing charge.

**Result:** The owner brought this specific detail to the vendor directly, and the fee was removed after a short conversation.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Gather the Documents
Pull together the contract you originally signed and the most recent invoice or updated terms.

### Step 2: Upload and Ask for a Plain-Language Summary First

```
I've uploaded a vendor contract and a recent invoice. Before anything
else, summarize in plain language what this contract actually
commits me to, pricing, terms, minimums, and any fees, so I
understand the full picture clearly.
```

### Step 3: Ask Claude to Flag Anything Unusual

```
Now compare the invoice against the original contract terms. Flag
anything that seems different from what was originally agreed, any
new fees, price changes, or terms that don't match what I signed.
```

### Step 4: Calculate the Real Financial Impact

```
This [specific term or fee] has changed. Based on our typical order
volume of [your real numbers], calculate roughly how much this
actually costs us per month and per year, not just the percentage change.
```

### Step 5: Draft Specific Questions for the Vendor

```
Based on what we found, help me write a short, professional list of
specific questions to bring to this vendor, focused on the exact
terms or fees that changed, not a vague general complaint.
```

### Step 6: Decide Whether to Negotiate or Look Elsewhere
With the real numbers in hand, you're in a much stronger position to decide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I don't have the original signed contract anymore. Can this still help?**
Yes, though it's more limited. Claude can still review a current invoice and flag anything that looks unusual on its face.

**Q: Is this a substitute for having a lawyer review a contract?**
No, especially for anything involving a major commitment.

**Q: What if the vendor pushes back and says the new fee was always disclosed somewhere?**
Ask Claude to help you locate the specific clause in question.

**Q: How often should I do this kind of review?**
Once or twice a year for ongoing vendor relationships, and anytime you receive a notice of renewal or a term change.

**Q: Can this same approach work for a lease or an insurance policy?**
The same general approach works for most dense business documents, but treat Claude's review as a helpful first pass for anything with major legal or financial stakes.
