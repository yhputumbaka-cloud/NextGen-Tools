## Why This Matters
When someone searches "tacos near me" or "best brunch in [city]," Google Business Profile decides whether your restaurant shows up at all, and whether the listing looks credible enough to tap. It's free, and for a local restaurant it's often the single highest-leverage thing you can do online before spending a dollar on ads.

Most owners claim their profile once and never touch it again: no description, a few outdated photos, hours that haven't been right since last winter. Google reads an incomplete profile as a sign the business might not be active, and ranks accordingly. *A complete, well-written profile does the opposite, it tells Google you're open, active, and worth showing to someone hungry and nearby.*

The setup takes about an hour, and you only do it once. After that, a few small updates a month keep it performing.

## Case Study: Marisol's Kitchen
Marisol's Kitchen had been open for two years but rarely showed up when locals searched for nearby dinner spots, despite a loyal regular crowd. The owner spent an afternoon completing the profile with Claude, rewriting a stale description, uploading fresh photos of the dining room and a few signature dishes, and building out the full menu inside the listing instead of linking to a PDF.

**Result:** Within two months, "directions requested" on the profile had roughly tripled, and the owner noticed several new faces mentioning they'd found the restaurant on Maps.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Claim Your Profile
Go to business.google.com and sign in. Search for your restaurant by name.
- Already exists? Click **"Claim this business."**
- Doesn't exist yet? Click **"Add your business."**

Google will verify ownership, usually by mailing a postcard with a code to your address, sometimes by phone or email instead.

> **Finish verification before doing anything else in this guide.** Nothing below matters until you're verified.

### Step 2: Fill In Every Basic Field Completely
Go through every section of your dashboard. Don't skip anything:
- Use your exact trading name, nothing extra stuffed in
- Pick the most specific category available, "Farm-to-Table Restaurant," not just "Restaurant"
- Confirm your address, and add a delivery service area if relevant
- Make sure your hours are accurate, including holidays
- Check every attribute that applies, outdoor seating, wheelchair access, takeout, reservations

Every completed field is a small signal to Google that you're real and active.

### Step 3: Write Your Description with Claude
Your description has a **750 character limit**, one of the few places you get to use real language to say what makes your restaurant worth a visit.

```
Write a Google Business Profile description for my restaurant.
Here are the details:

Name: [restaurant name]
Type of food: [cuisine]
Location: [neighborhood or city]
What makes us different: [2 to 3 things, e.g. family-owned,
wood-fired oven, locally sourced produce]
Atmosphere: [e.g. casual, date-night, family-friendly]

Keep it under 750 characters, write in third person, and
naturally work in the phrases "[city] restaurant," "[cuisine]
food," and "[a dish you're known for]."
```

Read what Claude gives you and push back if anything sounds off. Try "make the second sentence sound less generic" rather than starting over, Claude can keep adjusting the same draft with you instead of giving you a flat rewrite each time.

### Step 4: Add Photos That Actually Convert
Restaurants with **100+ photos** on their profile consistently get more direction requests and calls than those with only a handful.

You don't need a professional shoot. Upload:
- 5 to 10 photos of your most photogenic dishes, in natural light
- 2 to 3 interior shots during service
- At least one exterior shot, so people recognize the building when they arrive
- A photo of the team or kitchen, if you want some personality in there

Set your single strongest food photo as the cover image, that's what shows up first in search results.

*Tip:* If a photo feels flat or poorly lit, upload it directly into your Claude conversation and ask what's wrong with it and how you could improve the lighting next time you shoot. Claude can look at the actual image and give specific feedback, not generic photography tips.

### Step 5: Build Out Your Menu
Under the Menu section, link to an external PDF or build the menu directly inside the profile. Building it directly is the better move, Google can index individual dishes, so you can show up when someone searches a specific item by name.

```
Write short, appetizing Google Business Profile menu descriptions
for these dishes at [restaurant name]. Each one should be one to
two sentences, highlight the key ingredients, and sound appealing
without going overboard. Dishes: [list your items]
```

### Step 6: Turn On Messaging and Clean Up Q&A
- Turn on **Messaging** so customers can message you straight from the profile
- Set an auto-reply letting them know you'll respond within a few hours
- Check the **Q&A section**, anyone can post a question there, and if you don't answer it, a stranger might answer it wrong. Go through and answer anything outstanding yourself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My restaurant already has a listing but I did not create it. What do I do?**
That's a user-generated listing. Go to business.google.com, find it, and click "Claim this business." Once verified, you have full control to edit and update it.

**Q: How often should I actually update this?**
Update your hours *immediately* whenever they change, and add a new photo once or twice a month. Posting a Google Update once a week is a strong signal to Google that the profile is active.

**Q: Does having more reviews actually help my ranking?**
Yes, meaningfully, both review count and average rating are ranking factors. A line on your receipt or a small QR code near the register linking straight to your review page is a simple way to get more.

**Q: Can one account manage multiple locations?**
Yes. Each location needs its own separate verification and its own fully completed profile.

**Q: What is the actual difference between Google Business Profile and Google Maps?**
They're connected. Your Google Business Profile is what you manage on the back end. Google Maps is what the customer actually sees. Improving your profile improves how you show up on both.
