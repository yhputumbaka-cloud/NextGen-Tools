## What This Guide Covers
- How to take one promotion idea and develop it into **every piece of content it needs**, in a single ongoing conversation
- How to keep every piece consistent, since **Claude retains the full context** of the campaign as you go
- How to walk away with **captions, an email, an in-restaurant table card, and a staff briefing**, all from one sitting

---

## Why This Matters
A real promotion or campaign needs several pieces working together: customers need to see it on social media, hear about it by email, notice it on a table card, and have staff who can speak to it confidently. Building these pieces separately, in separate sessions, often leads to inconsistency.

---

## Case Study: Magnolia House
For a slow Tuesday-Wednesday stretch, the owner of Magnolia House wanted to run a midweek promotion but had always found campaign planning overwhelming. Working through one extended conversation with Claude, the owner walked away in under an hour with a complete, ready-to-use set of materials.

**Result:** Midweek covers increased noticeably during the promotion, and the owner kept the same workflow for two more campaigns afterward.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Define the Campaign Before Writing Anything

```
I want to plan a full campaign for a promotion at [restaurant name].
Here's the idea:

The offer: [e.g. 20% off all entrees Tuesday and Wednesday]
The goal: [e.g. boost midweek covers]
The dates it runs: [start and end date]
Our brand voice: [paste your voice guide or describe briefly]
```

### Step 2: Generate the Social Media Posts

```
Write three social media posts to promote this campaign: an
announcement post for when it launches, a reminder post for midway
through, and a last-chance post for the final day. Keep each one
under 100 words and include a clear call to action.
```

### Step 3: Generate the Email

```
Now write an email to our subscriber list announcing this same
campaign. Include a clear subject line, and make sure it reflects
the same offer and dates as the social posts.
```

### Step 4: Generate the In-Restaurant Table Card

```
Now write short copy for a table tent or card customers will see
in-restaurant during the promotion. It needs to be readable at a
glance, no more than two short lines plus the offer details.
```

### Step 5: Generate the Staff Briefing

```
Now write a short staff briefing explaining this promotion, what
the offer is, which dates it runs, and how to answer the two or
three questions customers are most likely to ask about it.
```

### Step 6: Do One Final Consistency Check

```
Look back over everything we've written for this campaign, the
social posts, the email, the table card, and the staff briefing.
Are the dates, the offer, and the tone consistent across all of them?
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to do all six steps in one sitting?**
It's the easiest way to keep everything consistent, but you can pause and return to the same conversation later, just remind Claude of where you left off.

**Q: What if I want to add a piece not covered here, like a flyer for a community board?**
Stay in the same conversation and ask for it the same way, the established context carries over.

**Q: How far in advance should I run this process before a campaign launches?**
At least a week, ideally two, so there's time to actually print materials and brief staff.

**Q: Can I reuse this same conversation for a different campaign later?**
Start a fresh conversation instead, reusing an old one risks blending details from the previous campaign.

**Q: What if the campaign doesn't perform the way I hoped?**
Bring the results back into a new conversation and ask Claude to help you think through why.
