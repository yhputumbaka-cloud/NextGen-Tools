## What This Guide Covers
- How to **role-play a difficult conversation** with Claude before having it for real, an underperforming employee, a vendor negotiation, a tense partner discussion
- How to use the practice round to **find the right words**, not just rehearse what you already planned to say
- How to walk into the real conversation **calmer and clearer** because you've already worked through the hard parts once

---

## Why This Matters
Running a restaurant means having hard conversations regularly. Practicing the conversation first, with something that can respond and push back like a real person might, makes a measurable difference.

---

## Case Study: Anchor & Vine
The owner of Anchor & Vine had been putting off a conversation with a kitchen manager whose performance had been slipping. Role-playing the conversation with Claude first, including a version where the manager responded defensively, helped the owner find a calmer, clearer way to open the conversation.

**Result:** The actual conversation went more smoothly than expected, and the manager responded constructively rather than defensively.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Describe the Real Situation Honestly

```
I need to have a difficult conversation and want to practice it first.
Here's the situation: [describe honestly, who it's with, what the
issue is, and what outcome you're hoping for].

Can you play the role of [the other person] in this conversation?
Respond the way you think they realistically might, including pushback
or defensiveness if that's likely, not just an easy, agreeable version.
```

### Step 2: Actually Start the Conversation
Type out how you'd genuinely open the real conversation, in your own words, not a polished version.

### Step 3: Let the Practice Conversation Run
Respond to Claude's in-character replies the way you would in real life. Stay in it rather than restarting immediately if it gets tense.

### Step 4: Step Out and Get Direct Feedback

```
Stepping out of the role-play now. How did that conversation go?
What did I do well, and where could I have been clearer, calmer, or
more direct?
```

### Step 5: Identify the One or Two Moments to Adjust
Focus on the specific moments that felt hardest, rather than trying to rewrite the whole conversation.

### Step 6: Run It Once More If It Would Help
There's no fixed number of rounds, stop once you feel clear and steady about the real conversation ahead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does this actually help, or does it just create a false sense of confidence?**
It helps most when you let the practice version include real pushback and tension, not just an easy, agreeable rehearsal.

**Q: What if the real conversation goes completely differently from how I practiced it?**
That's normal and still genuinely useful, the practice is about walking in with more clarity, not predicting exactly what will happen.

**Q: Is this only useful for conversations with employees?**
No, it works just as well for vendor negotiations or business partner disagreements.

**Q: Should I practice word for word, or just the general points I want to make?**
Both have value.

**Q: What if I don't have time to do this before every hard conversation?**
Reserve it for the conversations that genuinely warrant it, the ones with real stakes or real uncertainty.
