## What This Guide Covers
- Why a growing number of diners now ask **ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews** for restaurant recommendations instead of searching directly
- How to check whether your restaurant currently shows up when an AI assistant is asked about food in your area
- How to use Claude to **identify and fix the gaps** that are keeping you invisible to AI-driven search

---

## Why This Matters
For years, "getting found online" meant ranking well on Google. That's still true, but it's no longer the whole picture. A meaningful and growing share of people now ask an AI assistant directly and act on whatever it recommends, often without ever opening a traditional search results page.

AI assistants don't pull recommendations out of nowhere. They lean heavily on the same signals that matter for traditional search: a complete, accurate Google Business Profile, consistent information about your restaurant across the web, and a healthy volume of recent, detailed reviews. **If those signals are weak or inconsistent, you're effectively invisible in this new channel**, even if your food and service are excellent.

---

## Case Study: Pelican Point Seafood
Pelican Point had a strong Google presence but had never checked how it showed up in AI-driven search. Out of curiosity, the owner asked several AI assistants for seafood recommendations in the area. The restaurant didn't come up at all, while two competitors with weaker food but more complete, consistent online profiles did.

**Result:** After tightening up profile details and addressing a few outdated listings on other sites, the restaurant began appearing in AI-generated recommendations consistently, and the owner traced at least a handful of new reservations directly to it.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Test Your Current Visibility
Open a few different AI tools, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and a Google search that triggers an AI Overview, and ask natural questions a real customer might ask, "best [cuisine] restaurant in [neighborhood/city]," "where should I eat for [occasion] in [city]," and your own restaurant's name.

### Step 2: Audit Your Information for Consistency

```
I want to check that my restaurant's information is consistent
everywhere it appears online. Here's what's accurate:

Name: [restaurant name]
Address: [address]
Phone: [phone number]
Hours: [hours]

Help me build a short checklist of the places I should check this
against, things like my website, my Google Business Profile, and
any directory or review sites I'm listed on.
```

### Step 3: Strengthen the Signals AI Tools Actually Use
Beyond your Google Business Profile, focus on review volume and recency, review detail (reviews that mention specific dishes give AI tools more to work with than a bare star rating), and website clarity.

### Step 4: Encourage More Detailed Reviews

```
Write a short, friendly request I can say to a happy customer in
person, asking them to leave a Google review. Encourage them to
mention a specific dish or part of their experience, without making
the request feel scripted or transactional.
```

### Step 5: Make Your Distinctiveness Explicit

```
Based on what makes my restaurant distinct, [list 2 to 3 honest
differentiators], help me write one clear sentence that sums up
what we're known for.
```

Use that same sentence everywhere.

### Step 6: Re-Test Periodically
Every month or two, repeat Step 1 and ask the same AI tools the same questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is this really worth my time if most of my customers still just use Google Maps?**
The underlying fixes improve your traditional search ranking too. There's no real downside to doing this work.

**Q: I don't show up at all right now. Is that something I did wrong?**
Not necessarily. It usually reflects incomplete or inconsistent information rather than any single mistake.

**Q: Can I pay to show up in AI recommendations the way I can pay for ads?**
Not directly. The most reliable path right now is strengthening the same organic signals covered in this guide.

**Q: How is this different from the Google Business Profile guide?**
That guide focuses on one specific platform. This guide is broader, it's about how your restaurant's information appears and gets used across the wider web.

**Q: Will this be different again in six months?**
Likely yes, to some degree. The core habits, accuracy, consistency, detailed reviews, a clear identity, are durable even as the specific tools continue to shift.
