## What This Guide Covers
- How to research what nearby competitors are doing well, online and in AI search, without copying them
- How to use Claude to turn scattered observations into **clear, actionable gaps** you can act on
- How to walk away with **specific ideas**, not just a vague sense that "they're doing more than us"

---

## Why This Matters
Most restaurant owners have a rough sense of what their competitors are up to. But a rough sense rarely turns into anything actionable. The goal here isn't to copy a competitor's identity, it's to systematically notice what's working for them and figure out whether there's a real gap you could fill.

---

## Case Study: Anchor & Vine
The owner of Anchor & Vine suspected a newer competitor down the street was pulling weekend reservations away, but couldn't articulate exactly why. A structured review using Claude revealed that the competitor was posting behind-the-scenes kitchen content several times a week, and was responding to every single Google review within a day.

**Result:** Anchor & Vine adopted both habits in its own distinct voice, and weekend reservation numbers stabilized within two months.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Pick Two or Three Real Competitors
Choose restaurants that genuinely compete for the same customer, rather than the biggest name nearby regardless of fit.

### Step 2: Gather Raw Observations Yourself
Spend twenty minutes looking at each competitor's Google Business Profile, Instagram, and recent reviews. Jot down anything that stands out.

### Step 3: Bring Your Observations to Claude for Structure

```
I've been researching a few local competitors and want help turning
my notes into something actionable. Here's what I observed about
[competitor name]: [paste your raw notes].

What patterns do you see in what they're doing well? What might be
working for them, and why?
```

### Step 4: Ask for the Direct Comparison

```
Compare these competitors against what I do at [your restaurant
name]. Here's roughly how we currently approach social media,
reviews, and our online presence: [brief honest description].

Where are the clearest gaps between what they're doing and what
we're doing?
```

### Step 5: Separate "Worth Adapting" From "Not Right for Us"
Go through the list Claude generates and sort it honestly. Push back if a suggestion feels off.

### Step 6: Turn the Shortlist Into a Simple Action Plan

```
From the gaps we agreed are worth pursuing, help me build a simple
action plan, three things I could realistically start doing in the
next month, ranked by how much effort each one takes versus how
much it might help.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it ethical to research competitors this closely?**
Yes, this only uses publicly available information, the same things any customer could see.

**Q: What if a competitor is doing something that just seems impossible to match, like a much bigger ad budget?**
Note it and move on. This exercise is most valuable for finding gaps you can realistically close.

**Q: How often should I redo this kind of competitive check?**
Once or twice a year is reasonable for most independent restaurants.

**Q: Should I tell my staff or customers I'm doing this kind of research?**
There's no need to. This is background strategic work.

**Q: What if I genuinely can't find three real competitors nearby?**
Look slightly further afield, or compare against a restaurant outside your immediate area with a similar concept.
