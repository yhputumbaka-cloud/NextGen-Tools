## What This Guide Covers
- How to turn **staff availability and past patterns** into a realistic first-draft schedule
- How to handle common scheduling conflicts and constraints **without manually cross-checking** every name against every shift
- Why this guide produces a **starting draft to refine**, not a finished schedule to post without review

---

## Why This Matters
Building a weekly staff schedule is one of the most time-consuming recurring tasks for an independent restaurant owner. Claude can take on the first heavy lift, organizing availability and constraints into a coherent draft schedule, leaving you to focus your time on the judgment calls a tool can't make for you.

---

## Case Study: Founders Row Tavern
The manager at Founders Row Tavern spent close to three hours every week building the schedule by hand. Using Claude to build a first draft from the same availability information cut that initial build time dramatically.

**Result:** Weekly scheduling time dropped to under an hour, and the manager reported fewer last-minute conflicts.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Gather Real Availability and Constraints
Collect actual availability from your team and note any hard constraints, legally required breaks, maximum weekly hours, anyone who can't work a specific shift.

### Step 2: Give Claude the Full Picture

```
Help me build a draft staff schedule for next week. Here's our
staff and their availability: [list names and availability]. Here
are the shifts we need covered: [list shifts and how many people
each needs]. Here are any hard constraints: [list anything non-
negotiable, like required days off or legal hour limits].
```

### Step 3: Add Soft Preferences and Patterns

```
A few additional things to consider: [e.g. "Maria and Jake work well
together on the line," "Try not to schedule anyone for more than
four closing shifts in a row," "We're usually busier on weekend
dinner shifts and need our most experienced servers then"].
```

### Step 4: Review the Draft Critically, Not as a Final Answer
Check for anything that violates a constraint, anything unfair in hours distribution, and anything that doesn't match your real knowledge of your team.

### Step 5: Ask for Specific Adjustments

```
This draft has [specific person] scheduled for five closing shifts
in a row, which doesn't work. Can you adjust their schedule while
keeping everyone else's shifts the same?
```

### Step 6: Finalize and Post, With a Clear Channel for Conflicts
Post the schedule through whatever system your team already uses, and make clear there's a fast way to flag a conflict before the week starts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Should I trust the draft schedule enough to post it without changes?**
No. Treat every draft as a strong starting point that still needs your review.

**Q: What if my team's availability changes frequently week to week?**
That's normal, and this process handles it well since you're providing fresh availability each time.

**Q: Can this account for legal requirements around scheduling, like predictive scheduling laws?**
Mention any specific legal requirements directly in your prompt, and double check the draft yourself.

**Q: How far in advance should I build the schedule this way?**
Whatever timeline you currently use, most restaurants post one to two weeks ahead.

**Q: What if a staff member disagrees with their assigned shifts after the schedule is posted?**
Handle that the same way you would with any manually built schedule, a direct conversation.
